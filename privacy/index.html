PlainPocket Privacy Policy

Last updated: 25 November 2026.

In short: PlainPocket has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no third-party SDKs. The developer does not collect, receive, sell or share your data. Your ledger lives on your own devices, and the app does not send it anywhere. The only exceptions are the optional iCloud features described below, which use your own iCloud and never the developer's servers.

What PlainPocket stores, and where

  • Your ledger (accounts, transactions, categories, rules, budgets, settings): stored only on your device, in an encrypted database. The key is kept in your device's Keychain.
  • Apple Wallet accounts (optional, FinanceKit): if you choose "Sync Apple Wallet accounts", PlainPocket asks for access through Apple's own permission sheet and reads the transactions of the Wallet accounts you allow (such as Apple Card, Apple Cash and Savings, and, in the UK, banks added to Wallet). They are read on your device through Apple's FinanceKit framework and added to your on-device ledger. The developer never receives them; they are not sent to any server, not shared with anyone and not used for advertising or marketing. You can stop at any time in PlainPocket's settings or in Settings > Wallet.
  • Files you import (CSV, OFX/QFX, QIF, PDF statements): read on your device to create transactions. PlainPocket does not upload them.
  • Categorization: done on your device with PlainPocket's own rules, and optionally with Apple's on-device model. Nothing is sent to a server.
  • Camera (optional): used only when you scan a receipt. It is read on your device with Apple's Vision framework, and the photo is not stored or sent anywhere.
  • Face ID (optional): you can lock PlainPocket with Face ID or your passcode. This is handled by iOS; PlainPocket never receives your biometric data.
  • Notifications (optional): local reminders such as the end of your free trial, scheduled on your device.
  • iCloud sync (optional, off by default): if you turn it on, your ledger is copied to your own private iCloud (CloudKit) database under your Apple Account. The developer cannot see or access it.
  • Assistant: answers about your spending are worked out on your device from your own ledger, with PlainPocket's own logic and, where available, Apple's on-device model. Nothing is sent to a server.
  • Household (optional, off until you set it up): if you create or join a Household, PlainPocket publishes a small monthly summary to a shared iCloud zone: spending totals per built-in category, the balances of the accounts you choose to include, your monthly budgets and the name you pick. Up to three people can take part, invited by you through iCloud sharing. Merchant names, transactions, notes and custom categories are never shared. The data lives in your own iCloud under your Apple Account and is shared only with the people you invite; the developer cannot see or access it. Leaving or ending the Household deletes your summary from the shared zone.
  • Apple Watch, widgets and Live Activities: summaries (such as safe to spend) are shared between the app, widgets and your paired Apple Watch on your own devices. They do not leave them.
  • Exports and backups (you choose): CSV, JSON, Beancount and database snapshots are plain files that go only where you choose to save or share them.

Purchases

Subscriptions are processed by Apple through the App Store. The developer does not receive your payment details or your name. Manage or cancel subscriptions in Settings > Apple Account > Subscriptions.

Sharing

The developer does not sell, rent or share personal data. If you set up a Household, the summary described above is visible to the people you invite, and only to them. Packs you share (rules and bank layouts) contain no transactions and go only where you send them.

Tracking and analytics

PlainPocket does not track you across apps or websites and contains no analytics or advertising code. It does not use the network to send your data anywhere.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your data stays on your device until you delete it. Use "Delete all data" in Settings, or delete the app, to remove it. To remove an iCloud copy, turn sync off and delete it from your iCloud storage settings. To remove your Household data, use Leave or End in the Household screen. You can export everything at any time, even if your subscription lapses.

Children

PlainPocket is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect any personal information.

Changes
